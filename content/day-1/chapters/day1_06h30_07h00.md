## Most useful takeaways

- Individual memory preserves a specialist's role learning; a separate shared pool holds organization-wide knowledge. Group chats provide the deliberate point where those contexts meet.
- The broadcast makes a useful distinction between the work AI compresses and the work humans still own: deciding the product and its construction strategy remain judgment-heavy; task coordination is increasingly cheap.
- “Build for ourselves” is not yet a customer definition. The team has to choose between first-time organizers and established businesses using a pop-up for promotion because those users need different products.
- Agentic execution amplifies fuzzy product thinking. Without a sharp initial segment, a fast fleet will simply produce the most generic possible solution faster.
- Convert the idea into a tiny vertical workflow before assigning agents: decide the merchandise, register attendees with a social component, and find the venue. Time slots and broader platform features can wait.
- Low-fidelity browser-driven sketches are used as a conversation tool. The point is not polished design but something concrete enough for the humans to critique while they are still resolving the product.
- Shareable agents behave like templates rather than cloned identities. Reusable behavior and setup transfer, while private conversation state and secrets do not.
- A practical sign of excessive scope is that the bot becomes disoriented: responses stop making sense or consistently miss the target. The corrective action is to narrow its responsibility or clear polluted context.
