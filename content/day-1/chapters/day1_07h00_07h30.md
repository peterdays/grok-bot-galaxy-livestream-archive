## Most useful takeaways

- Build the internal tool that makes the first real event happen before attempting to turn it into a generic platform. Generalization is a later step based on observed primitives, not an assumption.
- A generative bot needs grounding. Instead of asking for unlimited merchandise concepts, give it real supplier/API inventory and ask it to choose a small number of feasible, high-quality options.
- Explicitly ask an ideation bot to move beyond restating the prompt. The presenters notice and correct a “regurgitation” failure rather than treating fluent repetition as progress.
- Voice capture is used to transfer a long, messy discussion into shared working context. A dedicated worker can then act on the recap without every detail being manually rewritten.
- Their recurring anti-scope-creep question is: what must exist for the experience to happen? In this case it is still only the venue, the merchandise, and the people/registration.
- The platform-business pattern is to satisfy a specific initial customer, discover robust primitives through use, and only then expose those primitives to adjacent groups.
- Prefer automation loops whose success can be inspected. For a creator, brand discovery, outreach, rate negotiation, and content ideation can be separated into workers with observable outputs; code is another naturally testable loop.
- Delegate the whole job, not an isolated middle step. The founder workshop explicitly includes execution and verification in the worker's responsibility.
- The highest-leverage founder benefit described here is reduced mental load: triage the information fire hose and hand off necessary-but-not-strategic work so focus remains available for high-quality decisions.

## Condensed narrative

The live team spends much of this half hour exploring collectible and physical merchandise, but several useful working habits emerge: ship the real event before platformizing, ground ideas in supplier constraints, limit the option set, and keep returning to the smallest required workflow. A founder interview adds a generalization lesson and a strong criterion for agent tasks—correct, verifiable output. At 07:23 the founder-focused workshop begins and reframes persistent bots as compounding workers responsible for end-to-end execution and verification.
