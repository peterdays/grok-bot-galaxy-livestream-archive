## Most useful takeaways

- Split a broad automation at concrete system boundaries. “Monitor ads and adjust spend” becomes authentication/data extraction, cross-source analysis, decision policy, and a separately controlled write action.
- Let agents prompt the operator. Requirements questions are valuable when they force domain experts to articulate state transitions, risks, and exceptions that tacit tool knowledge previously concealed.
- Domain expertise shifts upward rather than disappearing: systems intuition, taste, relationship-building, and problem definition become more important as button-clicking and glue code get cheaper.
- Do not expose production secrets for the sake of a live demo. The team keeps screens hidden while configuring credentials, a small but correct operational decision.
- Preview environments are part of the verification architecture. Their play-test bot opens each Vercel preview rather than waiting for a change to reach production.
- Development success is not production readiness. Launch adds X authentication, DNS and CORS behavior, database/load testing, ingress/egress restrictions, credential handling, and tighter security controls.
- The strongest incident pipeline produces an actionable code change: ingest a report, reproduce it, correlate logs, decide whether it is real, involve a domain owner if necessary, and open a PR. Merely summarizing the complaint or building another dashboard leaves the expensive step to a human.
