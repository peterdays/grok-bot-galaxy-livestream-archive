## Most useful takeaways

- Preserve sender trust in outbound work. Recognition and relevance matter more than exaggerated subject-line tricks; automating an unwanted message only scales the damage.
- Monetization often introduces intentional constraints—cooldowns, scarce currency, ads, or paid acceleration. Identify what players genuinely value before inserting friction, and measure whether it damages the core experience.
- Brainstorming and prioritization are separate stages. First capture ideas cheaply, then use explicit impact/effort scoring and human disagreement before asking engineering agents to build anything.
- A role label is not expertise. Give a specialist agent authoritative learning material, extract durable principles, turn those principles into persistent instructions, and then judge the resulting work.
- Encode tacit craft as testable rules. “Be a good data scientist” is vague; “use formulas unless a cell is an explicit input” is inspectable and reusable.
- Treat agents like new colleagues who need onboarding: company context, preferred tools, communication norms, examples of good work, and recurring feedback.
- A coordinator plus specialists can preserve both visibility and depth. Scheduled stand-ups provide the portfolio view; direct conversations handle nuanced tasks without forcing the coordinator to perform every specialty.
- The self-completing task list is an event-driven pipeline: ingest multiple inboxes, classify task versus FYI, choose a capable specialist, produce a first pass, and leave consequential action under human review.
- Policy-heavy operational decisions are good automation candidates when the rules are explicit and the evidence is auditable. Territory ownership is less about creative judgment than consistently mapping facts to rules of engagement.
- Giving one agent every personal and professional inbox is powerful but extremely high privilege. Use least-privilege connectors, source boundaries, logging, and approval gates; the speakers describe the convenience more than the security model.
