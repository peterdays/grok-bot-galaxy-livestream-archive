## Most useful takeaways

- Match the execution path to the task. A full browser VM is useful when no structured integration exists or visual interaction is the point, but it is needlessly expensive for work a direct API or product-native generator can do.
- Scheduled routines have a multiplicative cost: three checks every 15 minutes become hundreds of daily messages. Start with the slowest cadence that still meets the operational need, then increase it only with evidence.
- Do not create management layers in anticipation of scale. Keep coordinator-to-specialist routing flat until actual fan-out, context, or quality limits appear.
- The Q&A exposes an important reliability gap: the speaker does not claim that one specialist independently verifies another. A coordinator synthesizing multiple outputs is not the same as source-level or outcome-level verification.
- Treat claimed time and retention gains as anecdotal unless measured. The presenter reports shorter workdays and earlier customer interventions, but offers no controlled baseline or direct attribution.
- Portability needs explicit artifacts. “Export all context and rebuild” may be a reasonable migration experiment, but it is not a demonstrated path for preserving skills, tool permissions, schedules, provenance, or behavioral tests.
- Agent deletion can leave dependency debt. Maintain a registry of owners, callers, tools, routines, and downstream references; test those dependencies before retiring or merging a specialist.
- Preserve epistemic honesty. When asked whether Grokbot could host and route other coding agents, the presenter says she has not seen it done and will not claim success. The archive should retain that distinction rather than turning the idea into a capability claim.
- A funnel metric is only meaningful when the conversion event and user incentive are defined. Here, practice mode is useful without login, the X-login prompt is weakly placed, and the team correctly questions whether the displayed funnel represents a real product failure.
- The highest-leverage output of a one-off build may be its reusable production system. Their game studio accumulates design context, coding workflows, preview deployments, automated play tests, and feedback loops that can seed the next title.
