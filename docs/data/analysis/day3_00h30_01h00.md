## Most useful takeaways

- Replace policy documents and repeated reminders with a tool whose valid actions naturally enforce the process. The lead-review app makes sales follow-up easy while collecting structured reasons that marketing can use.
- A self-completing task list is a pipeline, not a smarter to-do app: monitor input channels on a schedule, separate FYIs from actions, infer the requested outcome from the full thread, route it to a specialist with domain context, then surface a draft for approval.
- Assign bots by stable responsibility and access. The inbox receiver knows communication channels; the chief of staff prioritizes and routes; the product bot probes requirements; the engineering bot understands the CRM, warehouse, codebase, and deployment conventions.
- Product discovery still matters. The product bot’s best contribution is not immediate code—it exposes decisions about when CRM state should change, what must work offline, and which actions require confirmation.
- Encode handoffs between roles. Once the requirements are resolved, the product bot writes the spec and contacts the engineering bot without a person copying text between chats.
- Separate build time from adoption time. The app took roughly ten hours to build, but two weeks to introduce, observe, and refine; organizational rollout remains real work even when code generation is fast.
- Desktop access can bridge missing APIs, but authentication becomes an operational concern. Monitor session validity, use a controlled credential vault, and require re-authentication rather than assuming a UI session is permanent.

## Condensed narrative

This marketing-operations talk is more practical than a generic product presentation. It shows a four-role bot team and two concrete systems. The first turns messages and calendar events into routed, partially completed work. The second turns an error-prone marketing/sales policy into a lead-review product that captures structured feedback in the CRM. The strongest live segment is the requirements dialogue: the product bot forces decisions about state transitions, offline behavior, and confirmations, then passes a written specification to engineering. The result illustrates both the speed of agent-assisted building and the slower human work of adoption and feedback.
