## Most useful takeaways

- Put one coordinator in front of a specialist team when chat and notification overhead would otherwise erase the benefit of delegation. The coordinator should route work and return one coherent result, while specialists remain inspectable.
- Separate expertise by durable responsibility: voice/style, factual source of truth, internal-news monitoring, follow-up production, and per-account memory. The split is useful only when ownership and handoffs are explicit.
- Keep consequential communication in draft mode. The demonstrated follow-up flow creates Gmail and Slack drafts and an account artifact, but the human still reviews and sends them.
- Make account context operational rather than archival. A per-account agent should know stakeholders, open promises, blockers, recent messages, and next actions, and be able to produce a consistent reset on demand.
- Use browser automation for bounded, verifiable artifacts. The ROI form is a stronger demo than a verbal claim because viewers can watch fields being created and inspect the finished form and links.
- Multi-agent discussion needs designed disagreement. Telling participants to challenge one another and protect different priorities is more useful than asking several agreeable models for parallel summaries.
- Begin automation discovery with a context dump, not a prematurely detailed org chart. A short spoken description of what is energizing, repetitive, broken, and easy to forget gives the system enough material to propose a first workflow.
- Treat self-improvement as a reviewed feedback loop: observe repeated manual work, compare the draft with the human's final edit, propose a rule change, and limit the rate of changes. The presenter's one-suggestion-per-week cap prevents routine and agent sprawl.
- Verify the difference between a staged demonstration and completed execution. The meeting-join flow visibly operates the browser, but the handoff is dismissed and the resulting recap is generated in demo-aware fallback behavior; it should not be counted as proof of unattended meeting capture.
- Preserve provenance for generated customer material. Drafts and account summaries should identify which transcript, notes, Slack threads, and account records support each claim, especially before external sending.

## Condensed narrative

This half hour presents a coherent customer-success agent system rather than a collection of isolated prompts. One chief-of-staff bot fronts specialists for writing style, grounded product answers, internal updates, follow-ups, and individual accounts. The strongest live sequence turns a finished customer call into review-only email and Slack drafts plus an ROI artifact, then demonstrates a browser-built intake form, an on-demand account reset, and a staff meeting in which agents are explicitly told to disagree over priorities. The setup advice is equally practical: begin with a spoken context dump, automate recurring checks, and improve the system by comparing generated drafts with what the human actually sent. The weekly cap on suggested improvements is an important guardrail against bot and routine proliferation. The meeting-attendance example is less conclusive than the other demos because the handoff is dismissed and a demo-aware fallback supplies the outcome, so it shows intended workflow more clearly than proven unattended execution.
