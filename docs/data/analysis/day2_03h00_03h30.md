## Most useful takeaways

- A hand-drawn product flow is sometimes faster and clearer than a written prompt. Keeping tldraw inside the coding environment lets the agent inspect the same visual artifact without a separate export/upload cycle.
- Hide complexity during play, not after it. Coarse visual strength and suspense improve the battle, while the result screen can reveal exact stats and why the outcome occurred for learning and sharing.
- Treat generated UI as a source of ideas rather than one indivisible proposal. They reject the incorrect main layout but preserve rank badges and post-game summaries that were not in the original sketch.
- Remove interaction that does not represent a decision. Round advancement can be timed automatically; a “next” button adds friction without agency.
- Design Mode creates a tight correction loop by attaching feedback to the exact rendered elements, which is more precise than describing “the cards near the top.”
- For unfamiliar animation technology, research and small independent prototypes are more appropriate than committing a large implementation. Keep game logic deterministic and animation as a replaceable presentation layer.
- Direct main-branch work is expedient for a single isolated contributor but becomes collision-prone immediately when several agents/humans edit together. Their eventual switch to PRs is the safer scalable pattern.
- Simple leaderboard scope—Elo order plus replay—is enough to close the social loop; medals/tiers can be layered on after the system works.
